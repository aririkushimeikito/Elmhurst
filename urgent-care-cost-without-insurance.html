How Much Does Urgent Care Cost Without Insurance?

How much does urgent care cost without insurance? Real self-pay ranges, what raises the bill, urgent care vs ER costs, and what to ask — Queens, NY.

By Keerthana Rajagopal, PA, Elmhurst Medical · Medically reviewed by Dr. Choudhury Hasan, MD · Updated August 2026

You are sick or hurt, you do not have insurance, and before you can even think about your symptoms you are doing math in your head. So let's answer the question directly: how much does urgent care cost when you are paying cash, what makes that number go up, and how do you find out the price before you are seen? None of this has to be a mystery.

How much does urgent care cost without insurance?

Self-pay pricing varies by clinic and by city, but published national ranges give you a realistic window. For a straightforward visit with no testing, the base evaluation typically runs about $150 to $280. Adding a rapid test brings a visit to roughly $175 to $350, and a visit that includes an X-ray commonly lands between $250 and $500 (Solv Health self-pay cost guide).

A visit with several tests plus wound care can reach $300 to $500 or more (Solv Health). For context, one insurer's national data put the average allowed cost of an urgent care visit around $193, versus $167 at a physician's office (UnitedHealth Group analysis, reported via U.S. News).

Two caveats. These are market ranges, not our prices — fees change, so call or text (718) 682-7090 and we will tell you the current self-pay cost before you come in. And "cheapest" is not always cheapest: a $180 visit that finds the problem beats a $60 visit that sends you elsewhere.

What makes an urgent care bill go up?

The visit fee usually covers the clinician's evaluation. Almost everything else is a separate line item.

Common add-ons and their typical self-pay ranges

Add-on service Typical self-pay range
Rapid strep or rapid flu test $25 – $75
COVID-19 rapid test $25 – $50
Urinalysis $30 – $60
Blood draw plus a basic panel $40 – $150
X-ray adds $100 – $250
Minor laceration repair (stitches) $150 – $250

Source: Solv Health self-pay urgent care cost guide.

Other things that move the total:

  • Procedures. Stitches, draining an abscess, splinting a sprain, or removing a foreign body take time and supplies.
  • Send-out labs. A swab read in the room is cheap; a culture or blood panel sent to an outside laboratory is billed by that lab, often separately — ask which lab and what their self-pay price is.
  • Prescriptions. Not part of your visit bill at all. Generics and pharmacy discount programs change what you pay dramatically.
  • Follow-up. A wound recheck or suture removal may or may not be included. Ask at the first visit.

Lab work can often be priced ahead of time. Published uninsured price lists from major reference labs show figures like a comprehensive metabolic panel around $22, hemoglobin A1c around $29, and a standard lipid panel around $51 (Quest Diagnostics uninsured patient price list), and Quest will email a self-pay estimate by test code on request (Quest Diagnostics).

Urgent care vs the emergency room: what's the real cost difference?

This is where uninsured patients get hurt financially, and the gap is not small.

Urgent care Hospital emergency department
Typical self-pay visit ~$150 – $500 depending on testing (Solv) ~$1,000 – $2,500 for common conditions when uninsured (Solv)
Average total visit cost ~$193 average allowed amount (UHG via U.S. News) $2,453 average ED visit (Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker)
Facility fee Uncommon (Peterson-KFF) Averaged $713 for evaluation and management in 2021, up 531% since 2004 (Peterson-KFF)
Best for Fevers, sore throats, urinary symptoms, sprains, minor cuts, rashes, ear pain Chest pain, trouble breathing, stroke symptoms, severe bleeding, major injury (ACEP)

For the same primary-care-treatable problem, treatment in a hospital emergency department averaged $2,032, roughly ten times the cost of an urgent care visit (UnitedHealth Group analysis, reported via U.S. News). About half of the average ED bill is the evaluation itself, and the fastest-growing piece has been the facility fee — the charge for walking through the door, which rose from $113 in 2004 to $713 in 2021 (Peterson-KFF).

None of that means avoid the ER when you need it. It means the ER is the right tool for a narrow set of problems and an expensive one for everything else.

What should you ask before you're seen?

You are allowed to ask about money at the front desk, and good clinics answer plainly. A script:

  1. "I'm paying cash. What is the self-pay price for a visit?"
  2. "If I need a test or an X-ray, what does each one add?"
  3. "Is lab work billed by you or by an outside lab? What's their self-pay price?"
  4. "Is a follow-up or a suture removal included?"
  5. "Do you offer a payment plan or a discount for paying today?"
  6. "Can I get a good faith estimate in writing?"

That last one is a legal right, not a favor. Under federal rules, uninsured and self-pay patients are generally entitled to a good faith estimate of expected charges when they schedule care or on request. If your final bill comes in at least $400 more than the estimate, you can dispute it through a federal patient-provider dispute resolution process (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services; CMS requirements).

Keep the estimate and photograph it. It is your best protection against a surprise number three weeks later.

What are your payment options if you can't pay it all today?

  • Cash or card at the visit. Many clinics have a discounted self-pay rate lower than the amount billed to insurance. Ask for it explicitly.
  • Payment plans. Ask before you are seen, not after the bill arrives.
  • Community health centers. Federally supported health centers see people whether or not they have insurance and charge on a sliding fee scale based on ability to pay (Health Resources and Services Administration). Search at findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov.
  • Public hospital financial assistance. NYC Health + Hospitals visits can cost as little as $0, and financial counselors are reachable at 1-844-692-4692 (NYC Health + Hospitals).
  • Coverage you may not know you qualify for. NYC Care and the Essential Plan may apply — both are covered in our guide to seeing a doctor without insurance in Queens.
  • Hospital charity care. New York State requires hospitals to offer financial assistance to patients who are uninsured, have exhausted coverage, or have paid medical expenses exceeding 10% of income (New York State Department of Health).

When to see a doctor about this

Cost should never be the reason you sit at home with a dangerous symptom. Skip urgent care and call 911 for:

  • Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes, or coming and going, with or without cold sweat, nausea, or pain in the jaw, neck, back, arm, or shoulder (CDC)
  • Sudden numbness or weakness of the face, arm, or leg — especially on one side — sudden confusion or trouble speaking, sudden trouble seeing, sudden trouble walking or loss of balance, or a sudden severe headache with no known cause (CDC stroke signs)
  • Trouble breathing, fainting or sudden weakness, uncontrolled bleeding, severe or sudden pain, coughing or vomiting blood, persistent severe vomiting or diarrhea, confusion, or thoughts of suicide (American College of Emergency Physicians)

Do not drive yourself for stroke symptoms — call 9-1-1 (CDC). If you come to urgent care with something genuinely serious, we stabilize you and arrange emergency transfer, which adds time to your care (ACEP). For fevers, sore throats, urinary symptoms, sprains, minor cuts, rashes, ear pain, and most infections, urgent care is the right and far less expensive door.

Frequently asked questions

How much does urgent care cost with no insurance for a simple visit?+

A basic evaluation with no testing typically runs about $150 to $280 nationally, and a visit with one rapid test about $175 to $350 (Solv Health). Ask the clinic for their exact self-pay rate before you are seen.

Is urgent care cheaper than the ER without insurance?+

Almost always, and by a wide margin. Uninsured ER visits for common conditions commonly run $1,000 to $2,500, while the average emergency department visit overall costs $2,453 (Solv Health; Peterson-KFF).

Can I get a price in writing before my visit?+

Yes. As an uninsured or self-pay patient you are generally entitled to a good faith estimate when you schedule or upon request, and you can dispute a bill that exceeds it by $400 or more (CMS).

How much do stitches cost without insurance?+

Minor laceration repair typically runs about $150 to $250 on top of the visit fee (Solv Health). Whether suture removal is included varies by clinic, so ask.

Will an X-ray be included in the visit price?+

Usually not. An X-ray commonly adds $100 to $250, bringing a typical visit with imaging to roughly $250 to $500 (Solv Health).

Can I pay less for blood work if I'm uninsured?+

Often yes. Published uninsured lab prices can be low — around $22 for a comprehensive metabolic panel and $29 for an A1c on one posted list — and you can request a self-pay estimate by test code (Quest uninsured price list; Quest self-pay estimate).
