The signature · the bloom

One flower for a whole community.

The marigold is celebratory and sacred in South Asian culture (genda) and the heart of Latino tradition (cempasúchil). It's our bridge — not clinical blue-and-white.

What it's made of

Marigold petals — warmth, optimism, welcome.
Teal center — the health & healing cue.
Coral heart — human care at the core.

How to use it

As a logo mark, an app/social icon, a stamp on forms, a bullet or divider, a loading animation, and a physical motif in the waiting room. Keep it full-color on light or dark grounds; don't recolor the petals.

✓ Do

  • Use cream as the default background — warm, not stark white.
  • Put white text on forest, teal or coral fills.
  • Use marigold as a highlight/fill behind dark or large text.
  • Reserve coral as a spark, not a main color.

✕ Don't

  • Don't set small marigold or coral text on cream (fails contrast).
  • Don't introduce clinical blue or pure #FFFFFF grounds.
  • Don't put forest text on teal or coral (too low contrast).
  • Don't use more than 2–3 fills in one layout.

Accessibility: forest and teal carry white text at WCAG AA. Marigold, coral and sage are fills behind dark or large text only. Everything is sized generously for older readers.

Hierarchy & scale

H1 ~44pt · H2 ~28pt · H3 ~16pt (Bricolage, tight tracking). Body 11pt / 1.6 (Jakarta). Captions 8–9pt in slate muted. Use Fraunces italic only for short accent phrases.

Free & easy to get

All four faces are free on Google Fonts. In Canva, use Bricolage Grotesque, Plus Jakarta Sans, Fraunces and Hanken Grotesk. Keep line length ~50–65 characters.

08
Voice & tone

Warm, plain, and human.

We write the way a trusted neighbor who happens to be a doctor would talk — clear enough for a 10th-grade reader, in any language.

Plain

Short sentences. Everyday words. Explain any medical term the first time you use it.

Warm

Lead with the patient's real worry, not the service. Reassure; never scare or shame.

Belonging

Multilingual by default. Speak to the whole family and every generation.

✓ Sounds like us

  • "Talk to a doctor who has time for you."
  • "In the language you speak at home."
  • "Not sure if it's worth a visit? It probably is."
  • "New patients welcome — call, text, or book online."

✕ Not us

  • "Utilize our comprehensive care modalities."
  • Dense clinical jargon with no explanation.
  • Fear-based or guilt-based messaging.
  • Corporate, distant, or over-promising claims.
One line to remember
Meet people where they are — in their language, with respect, and without the wait.

Imagery & photography

Real people, warm light.

Photography should look like Elmhurst actually looks — diverse, multigenerational, candid and respectful. Warm, natural light on our sage/cream/sand tones.

Diverse, real patients
Warm natural light
Candid, not posed
Sage / cream backdrops

✓ Do

  • Show real providers & patients of the community (South Asian, Latino, East Asian, all ages).
  • Use soft, warm lighting and generous space.
  • Add the bloom mark or a marigold accent as a consistent motif.
  • Round photo corners to match the UI (16–24px).

✕ Don't

  • Cold blue-toned or sterile clinical stock.
  • Obvious, generic stock handshakes / stethoscope close-ups.
  • Heavy filters that shift skin tones.
  • Cluttered, low-contrast compositions.

Illustration & icons: simple, rounded, friendly line/emoji-style marks that echo the bloom's soft geometry — never thin, corporate, or overly technical.

Flyer / half-page handout (portrait)
Do & don't recap
Flower up top. Big headline. Languages + phone at the bottom. Cream or white ground.

Content order that converts

1) Who it's for → 2) What we offer → 3) Why us (language, hours, price) → 4) How to reach us. Always end with the phone number and Zocdoc.

Keep it accessible

Minimum 10–11pt body for print, high contrast, and generous spacing — many readers are older or reading in a second language.
